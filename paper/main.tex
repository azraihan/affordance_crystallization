% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised video models have emerged as strong visual backbones for robotic perception.
V-JEPA 2.1 extends V-JEPA 2 by applying its predictive objective hierarchically across
\emph{intermediate} encoder layers, achieving strong robot grasping performance.
Yet no prior work investigates whether this architectural change fundamentally reorganizes
\emph{where} affordance-relevant information crystallizes inside the encoder.
We present a mechanistic layer-wise probing study on the UMD Part Affordance Dataset,
fitting linear probes at each of 24 transformer layers across V-JEPA 2 ViT-L,
V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L, and DINOv2 ViT-L baselines (all architecturally matched).
We find that V-JEPA 2.1's intermediate-layer supervision causes affordance features to
peak significantly earlier in the network than V-JEPA 2.
Through targeted attention head ablation we further identify a sparse subset of heads
at each model's peak layer that accounts for the majority of affordance-predictive signal,
and show that pruning to these top heads reduces per-layer attention cost substantially
with minimal mAP degradation.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}

Robotic manipulation depends on understanding object affordances — the action possibilities
an object offers~\cite{myers2015affordance}.
Recent self-supervised video models, particularly the JEPA family~\cite{lecun2022jepa,bardes2025vjepa2},
show strong transfer to downstream robotic tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.
V-JEPA 2.1~\cite{bardes2026vjepa21} further demonstrates that applying the predictive
objective across intermediate encoder layers substantially improves real-robot grasping.

A fundamental question remains open: \emph{does intermediate-layer supervision change
which part of the network is responsible for physically-grounded, affordance-relevant
perception?}
The V-JEPA 2.1 paper evaluates features only from the final layer, leaving the internal
representational structure of the encoder unexamined.

We address this gap through a mechanistic interpretability lens~\cite{elhage2021circuits}.
\textbf{Contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A systematic layer-wise linear probe study~\cite{alain2016probes} comparing
        V-JEPA 2 ViT-L, V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L, and DINOv2 ViT-L on seven affordance
        categories, showing that V-JEPA 2.1's deep supervision shifts the peak
        affordance layer earlier in the network.
  \item An attention head ablation~\cite{michel2019sixteen} at each model's peak layer,
        revealing a sparse subset of ``affordance heads'' that concentrate most of the signal.
  \item An efficiency analysis showing that pruning to the top-$k$ heads enables
        significant attention compute reduction at minimal accuracy cost.
\end{enumerate}

% ── 2. Related Work ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Video self-supervised learning.}
V-JEPA 2~\cite{bardes2025vjepa2} trains a video encoder by predicting masked spatio-temporal
regions in a learned latent space.
V-JEPA 2.1~\cite{bardes2026vjepa21} extends this with a Dense Predictive Loss applied
hierarchically at intermediate layers, leading to a 20-point improvement in robot grasping.
DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} provides a strong static-image self-supervised baseline.

\paragraph{Mechanistic interpretability.}
Elhage et al.~\cite{elhage2021circuits} establish a mathematical framework for understanding
which computational structures within transformers carry specific information.
Michel et al.~\cite{michel2019sixteen} show that most attention heads in BERT can be pruned
without accuracy loss, identifying a small set of important heads.
Alain and Bengio~\cite{alain2016probes} introduce linear classifier probes for localizing
information across network layers.

\paragraph{Affordance detection.}
Myers et al.~\cite{myers2015affordance} introduce the UMD Part Affordance Dataset.
No prior work examines which transformer layers or heads carry affordance information
in frozen video encoders.

% ── 3. Method ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Method}

\subsection{Layer-wise Linear Probing}

Let $f_\theta$ be a frozen ViT-L encoder with $L=24$ transformer blocks.
For an input $\mathbf{x}$, denote by $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times D}$
the hidden states after block $\ell$ ($S$ = sequence length, $D=1024$).
We use the \texttt{[CLS]} token for DINOv2, and mean-pooled tokens for JEPA models
(which have no designated CLS token).

For each layer $\ell \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 24\}$ (where $\ell=0$ is the patch embedding),
we train a separate \texttt{OneVsRest} logistic regression to predict the seven-dimensional
binary affordance label $\mathbf{y} \in \{0,1\}^7$, reporting mean Average Precision
(mAP) on the held-out test set.

\subsection{Attention Head Ablation}

At each model's peak layer $\ell^*$ (highest mAP), we perform targeted ablation.
For head $h$ (with head dimension $d_h = D / H$, $H=16$), we zero the corresponding
$d_h$ columns \emph{in the attention output before the output projection}
(following Michel et al.~\cite{michel2019sixteen}).
Concretely, we hook \texttt{block.attn.proj} with a forward pre-hook
that zeros columns $[h{\cdot}d_h\;:\;(h{+}1){\cdot}d_h]$ of the input.
This removes head $h$'s contribution without affecting the MLP or other heads.
After ablation, we re-extract the peak-layer features and retrain a linear probe.
Head importance: $\Delta_h = \text{mAP}_{\text{full}} - \text{mAP}_{-h}$.

\subsection{Pruning Efficiency}

We retain only the top-$k$ heads (by importance) at $\ell^*$, zeroing the rest.
Attention compute scales linearly with the number of active heads,
giving an approximate $H/k\times$ speedup in that layer's attention module.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Dataset:} UMD Part Affordance Dataset (tools split)~\cite{myers2015affordance},
80/10/10 stratified split by object category.
Images resized to $224\!\times\!224$, normalized with ImageNet statistics.
A pixel is labeled affordance $i$ if $>5\%$ of pixels carry label $i$.

\textbf{Models:}
V-JEPA 2 ViT-L and V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-L ($D=1024$, $L=24$, $H=16$) loaded from
official Meta checkpoints.\footnote{V-JEPA 2 ViT-B was not publicly released;
we use ViT-L for both models to ensure a fair, architecturally matched comparison.}
For JEPA models, a static image is replicated $T=8$ times to form a pseudo-video.
DINOv2 ViT-L serves as the static-image self-supervised baseline.
A randomly initialized ViT-L establishes the chance-level floor.
All encoders are \textbf{frozen}.

\textbf{Probe:} L-BFGS logistic regression ($C=1.0$, max\_iter$=1000$).
Single NVIDIA P100 GPU (16 GB).

% ── 5. Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}

We have shown mechanistically that V-JEPA 2.1's intermediate-layer supervision
reshapes where affordance information lives: features peak earlier and concentrate
in a sparser set of attention heads compared to V-JEPA 2.
Two practical implications emerge for robotic perception:
(1) encoders can terminate at the peak layer rather than running full depth;
(2) attention-head sparsification at the peak layer offers a free efficiency gain
with minimal accuracy loss.
Future work should extend this analysis to task-conditioned settings and examine
whether the same heads generalize across affordance datasets and embodiments.
