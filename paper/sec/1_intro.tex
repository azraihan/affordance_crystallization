\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Robotic manipulation requires understanding object affordances --- the action possibilities
an object offers~\cite{myers2015affordance}.
Self-supervised video models, especially the JEPA family~\cite{lecun2022jepa,bardes2025vjepa2},
transfer strongly to robotic tasks without task-specific fine-tuning.
V-JEPA~2.1~\cite{bardes2026vjepa21} introduces a Dense Predictive Loss applied
hierarchically at intermediate encoder layers, yielding a 20-point grasping improvement
over V-JEPA~2 --- yet evaluates only final-layer features, leaving internal representational
structure unexamined.

Prior mechanistic interpretability work~\cite{alain2016probes,elhage2021circuits} shows
that specific layers and attention heads carry task-relevant information, and that most
heads can be pruned without accuracy loss~\cite{michel2019sixteen} --- but this has
never been studied for affordance features in frozen video encoders.
DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} provides our static-image self-supervised baseline.

We make three contributions:
(1) a layer-wise linear probe study (layers 0--24) comparing V-JEPA~2, V-JEPA~2.1,
and DINOv2 ViT-L on seven affordance classes, showing that intermediate-layer supervision
shifts the peak affordance layer earlier;
(2) an attention head ablation at each model's peak layer, revealing a sparse set of
``affordance heads''; and
(3) a pruning efficiency analysis showing significant compute reduction at minimal accuracy cost.
