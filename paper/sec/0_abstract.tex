\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised video models are strong backbones for robotic perception.
We probe 25 layer positions of architecturally matched ViT-L encoders
(V-JEPA~2, V-JEPA~2.1, DINOv2) on the UMD Part Affordance Dataset and show that
\emph{video} SSL models crystallize affordance signal at layer~19---four layers earlier
than DINOv2 (image SSL, layer~23), suggesting a consistent effect of temporal predictive
objectives on representation depth.
Both V-JEPA variants share the same peak depth, yet V-JEPA~2.1's intermediate-layer
supervision yields markedly stronger early-layer representations
(layer~2 mAP: $92.2\%$ vs.\ $85.0\%$ for V-JEPA~2).
Head ablation at each peak layer further reveals a sparse set of attention heads that
concentrate the affordance signal; keeping only the top-3 heads achieves a
$5.3{\times}$ attention compute reduction with ${\leq}0.1\%$ mAP loss.
\end{abstract}
